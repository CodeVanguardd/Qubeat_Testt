How To Play

QuBeat is a beat-timed coding quiz duel — answer fast, hit your cues on the beat, and keep your HP above zero longer than your rival.

Game Flow

Here's every screen you'll pass through on your way into a match, in order.

1

Mode Select

First you pick a mode: Arcade (you vs an NPC) or Logic Duel (two players, pass-and-play). Click a mode to preview its description on the right in the Mode Preview panel.

Whichever mode you pick reveals its own Pacing option — Logic Duel shows Whole Note / Off-Beat Note / Ghost Note, Arcade shows Turtle / Normal / Swift / Blitz / Lightning. Pick your pace, then hit Continue — or Main Menu to back out.

2

Language Lab

Next is the Language Lab — pick a language card from the grid (HTML, CSS, Java, JavaScript, C, C# and more). The tip box on the left reminds you to master a language's basics before jumping into the coding challenges.

After clicking a language, a description pops up along with the option to Play or Learn More. Use Back anytime to return to Mode Select.

3

Choose Your Difficulty

Picking a language opens its difficulty picker — choose between Syntax, Compiler, and Overclock (see the breakdown below). A Syntax Guide link is available if you want a refresher first, and Begin carries you forward once you're ready.

4

Character Selection

Each player picks a character to play as. In Arcade, only you pick — the NPC's character is assigned automatically. In Logic Duel, both players pick their own before the match begins.

The tip box is here too: you can jump to Mode Select anytime — even mid-match — to switch mode, language, or difficulty. Back returns you to the difficulty picker.

5

Game Starting

The last stop before the action — a "Press Start to begin the game" prompt with your final Start and Back buttons. The Mode Select tip is still on hand in case you want to double-check your setup before diving in.

6

Results

Once the match ends, the Results screen shows who won and why — HP hitting zero, questions running out, or time running out — plus each player's final HP as a side-by-side bar. From here you can Play Again with the same settings or head back to the Main Menu.

The Match Loop

1

Pick a Question SHOOT

The picker chooses a question from the answers on screen. Time it to the beat — click on the "SHOOT" cue, not too early, not too late.

2

Answer Correctly

The other player (the answerer) picks the correct choice out of four. Get it right to fire back at the picker.

3

Defend the Hit PARRY

The picker must "PARRY" on beat to block the answerer's counter. A clean parry swaps roles for the next round — a missed one costs HP and the picker goes again.

4

Win the Duel

Wrong answers, missed cues, and blocked shots chip away at HP. First player to hit 0% HP loses the match.

In-Match HUD Preview

Player 1 / Player 2 HP (100%) — the two colored bars on the left and right edges of the top panel. Each shows how much health that player has left; it drains with every missed beat or wrong answer, and hitting 0% ends the match.

Mode — Logic Duel — the active game mode for this match (two players, each with their own character, as opposed to the single-player Arcade mode).

Pacing — Whole Note — how much reaction time you get on SHOOT/PARRY cues. This is one of the slower pacing settings, giving more time to react than faster settings like Lightning.

Language — Html — the quiz track this match is pulling questions from. It can be switched anytime from Mode Select.

Difficulty — Syntax — the difficulty tier of the questions being asked, affecting how tricky they are.

Time / Beat bar — the horizontal meter in the middle showing how close the next beat is; the number underneath ("2") counts down the current beat. Timing your click to this pulse — not just watching the number — is what lands a clean SHOOT or PARRY.

Pause button — top-left button to pause the match at any time.

Tip box — a reminder that you can open Mode Select anytime, even mid-match, to change the mode, language, or difficulty.

Character Display panel — right side panel showing the active character sprite and whose turn it currently is ("Player 1's Turn").

"Pick a Question" panel — the four color-coded answer tiles at the bottom. This is the picker's turn to choose which question gets asked (e.g. bold text elements, valid color names, largest heading, or the correct image element) — timed to the SHOOT cue on the beat bar above.

Game Modes

  • Arcade — you vs an NPC rival. Only you pick a character; the NPC's is assigned automatically and the match jumps straight to Start.
  • Logic Duel (Co-op) — two players, each picking their own character before the match begins.

Pacing & Difficulty

  • Pacing sets how long you get to react to SHOOT/PARRY cues — from Turtle (slow) up to Lightning (near-instant).
  • Difficulty changes how tricky the questions are, from basic syntax to advanced logic.

Choose Your Language

Quiz questions are pulled from your chosen language track — HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and more. Switch it anytime from Mode Select.

Health & Misses

Every missed beat or wrong answer costs HP, and repeated misses in a row deal escalating damage — so a shaky streak gets punished harder the longer it runs. Land your cues to keep your streak clean.

Tips

Watch the beat meter, not just the countdown number — timing your click to the pulse matters more than reacting fast. You can jump to Mode Select anytime, even mid-match, to change the mode, language, or difficulty.

About Pacing

Pacing sets how much reaction time you get on SHOOT/PARRY cues. Each mode has its own set of pacing options, ordered from most forgiving to most demanding:

Logic Duel (Co-op)

Whole Note

The slowest, most forgiving pacing — steady beats with plenty of room to react.

Off-Beat Note

A middle tier that shifts the rhythm slightly, so you have to pay closer attention to the beat meter.

Ghost Note

The trickiest Logic Duel pacing — cues are tighter and less predictable, rewarding sharp timing.

Arcade

Turtle

The slowest Arcade pacing — the most reaction time against the NPC.

Normal

A standard, balanced pace for a regular match.

Swift

A quicker pace that tightens your reaction window.

Blitz

Fast pacing with very little margin for hesitation.

Lightning

The fastest Arcade pacing — near-instant cues for players who want maximum challenge.

You can pick pacing on Mode Select before a match starts, and it's carried through with your other settings when you replay.

Difficulty

After picking a language, you'll choose a difficulty for its questions — from easiest to hardest:

Syntax

The easiest tier — questions focus on the basics, like fundamental syntax and core concepts of the language.

Compiler

A step up in difficulty — questions get trickier and expect a firmer grasp of how the language actually works.

Overclock

The hardest tier — the most challenging questions, for players who want to push their knowledge to the limit.

Difficulty is picked per language on the difficulty screen, alongside a Syntax Guide link if you want a refresher before you Begin.

FAQ

How is the winner decided?

By final HP — whoever has the higher HP wins the match. This applies whether the match ends because a player's HP hits zero, the questions run out, or time runs out. Equal HP is a tie.

What's the difference between Arcade and Logic Duel?

Arcade is you vs an NPC — only you pick a character, and the match jumps straight to Start. Logic Duel (Co-op) is pass-and-play for two players, and both players pick a character before the match begins.

Can I change the mode, language, pacing, or difficulty mid-match?

Yes — the in-match tip box is a reminder that you can open Mode Select at any time, even mid-match, to change these settings.

What happens if I miss a cue or answer wrong?

Every missed beat or wrong answer costs HP, and repeated misses in a row deal escalating damage — so a shaky streak gets punished harder the longer it runs.

Can I switch the quiz language later?

Yes — quiz questions are pulled from your chosen language track (HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and more), and you can switch it anytime from Mode Select.
